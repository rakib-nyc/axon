# AXON — A4b / A4c (readout gate now the architecture)

Readout gate implemented: `P.useBias = learn ? 1 : 0`. Bias is a learning-time
mechanism only. All references and readouts are now recorded bias-OFF.
**This moves the baseline** — under the gate the clamped reference is the no-bias
one, so recall is the 0.30–0.33 column from A4, not 0.21–0.24.

Throughput: **2,865 steps/s** (trained 40ep), 2,508 (untrained), 2,787 (120ep).

## A4b — the recalled set DRIFTS. Stop condition met.

Pairwise Jaccard of recalled neuron sets across cue levels, same stimulus:

| pair | trained | untrained |
|---|---|---|
| 100%/75% | 0.220 | 1.000 |
| 100%/35% | 0.217 | 1.000 |
| 100%/15% | 0.224 | 1.000 |
| 75%/35% | 0.218 | 1.000 |
| 75%/15% | 0.212 | 1.000 |
| 35%/15% | 0.216 | 1.000 |
| **mean** | **0.218** | **1.000** |

A fixed subset would give 1.000. The untrained control *does* give 1.000, so the
metric is capable of registering stability — **0.218 is a real drift, not an artifact.**

| | assembly | CORE (recalled at all 4 cues) | periphery |
|---|---|---|---|
| trained | 1536 | **45 (3.0%)** | 1491 |
| untrained | 1536 | 1488 (96.8%) | 48 |

### Core vs periphery — hypothesis directionally supported, magnitude too small

| group (trained) | n | in-degree | % synapses still at 5/6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| CORE | 545 | **763.7** | **35.6** |
| periphery | 17,887 | 707.8 | 42.9 |
| all neurons | 114,688 | 672.4 | 48.7 |

Both metrics move the predicted way — the core has more functional recurrent synapses
and more plasticity coverage. But the gap is **+7.9% in-degree and −7.3 points untouched**,
which does not plausibly explain a 3% / 97% recall split. "Periphery is simply
undertrained" is supported in direction, not in magnitude.

### Important caveat on the untrained 1.000

Untrained stability is **degenerate**: that network ignores the cue entirely and emits the
same collapsed state at every cue level (best-other 0.980). So high cue-invariance is not
automatically good. The trained network's recalled set *varies with cue* while keeping
best-other at 0.000 — stimulus-specific but not cue-invariant. The drift is at least partly
cue-dependence, not pure noise.

## A4c — bias does NOT diverge; it equilibrates

| | 40 epochs | 120 epochs |
|---|---|---|
| mean | 3235 | **3085** |
| p50 | 3453 | 3153 |
| p99 | 6235 | 5455 |
| max | **8287** | **7433** |

Mean and max are **lower at 120 epochs than at 40** — the bias reaches a steady state
around mean ~3100, max ~7400. Headroom to uint32 saturation is 5.8e5x. **No numerical
risk, no bound needed.**

### Correction to an earlier claim

In notes/04 I wrote that the bias "diverges monotonically." That is wrong — it
equilibrates. The pathology was never unbounded growth; it was the *magnitude* of the
equilibrium relative to drive: bias_eq/16 = 194 against a drive scale of ~65, ratio **3.1**,
which is the veto regime. Under the readout gate that ratio is now intended (it is what
drives allocation) and only applies during learning.

## Two incidental findings

1. **Untrained control is contaminated by exactly one learning presentation.** The
   `if (msN==0)` guard I added to avoid a nan in the timing report runs one presentation
   with `learn=true`. That is why untrained shows 93.2% (not 100%) of synapses at the
   initializer peaks. 1 presentation against 480 — small, but it should be a timed
   non-learning presentation instead.
2. **Allocator capacity.** 12 stimuli consumed 18,440 of 114,688 recurrent neurons as a
   near-exact partition. Extrapolating, the pool is exhausted at **~74 stimuli**, after
   which no unvetoed neurons remain. Capacity here is set by *allocation*, not by
   synaptic interference — an unusual property worth stating explicitly.
